- Founder, [Visit Dzaleka](https://visit.dzaleka.com/) and Dzaleka Digital Heritage

## Background

I was born in Burundi and grew up in Malawi, where I lived in a UNHCR refugee camp. I came to Australia in 2015 after being granted asylum by the Australian government.

At Networking African-Australians I help run homework clubs across Melbourne and regional Victoria, a mental health mentoring program, and leadership camps. In April 2025 we took 34 African-Australian students to Mount Eliza for three days on confidence, teamwork, mental health, and cultural identity. In September 2026, Ipswich City Council [appointed me as one of six external advisors](https://www.ipswich.qld.gov.au/News-Articles-Folder/2026/External-advisors-to-strengthen-work-of-Multicultural-Advisory-Committee), chosen from 34 applicants, to its Multicultural Advisory Committee. In that role I advise council on planning, policy, and services that affect the city's multicultural communities.

I've been starting things since 2016, when I founded Hungry Innovators, a non-profit for STEM education. In August 2019 I started Mentors Outreach in Brisbane. It is now mentoring program management software that organisations use to run applications, matching, sessions, safeguarding, and reporting, and Networking African-Australians runs its Homework Club on it. From 2018 to 2019 I hosted the Transition Phase Podcast, interviewing Brisbane entrepreneurs and creatives about how they built their work.

Much of my recent work points back to Dzaleka Refugee Camp in Malawi. [Dzaleka Online](https://services.dzaleka.com/) publishes news, stories, and service information from the camp; it recorded 17,952 active users in 2025, and in July 2026 I released its iPhone app. Visit Dzaleka runs guided tours led by residents trained as certified guides. Dzaleka Digital Heritage archives the camp's cultural history. I'm also building [DzalekaPay](https://pay.dzaleka.com/), which lets businesses and organisations in the camp take mobile money and card payments through payment links, tickets, storefronts, and fundraising pages.

I write software, too. I maintain four open-source projects: [ATO Lens](https://github.com/realbakari/ATO-Lens), a local-first tool for making sense of Australian tax records; [Capsule](https://github.com/realbakari/capsule), a control plane for running coding agents under explicit policy; [Capsule App](https://github.com/realbakari/Capsule-App), a desktop workspace where those agents' diffs, approvals, and checkpoints live on your own machine; and [UpDeck](https://github.com/realbakari/UpDeck), an unofficial dashboard for Up bank that works out payday, subscriptions, and spending on your device.

Away from work I read a lot and keep [summaries of the books](/book-summaries/) that stay with me. I also keep an [archive of sermons](/the-message/), and I make generative art with P5.js.

The [journey](/journey/) page lists all of this by year. You can reach me by [email](mailto:bakari@bakarimustafa.com) or on [Twitter](https://twitter.com/realbakari).
